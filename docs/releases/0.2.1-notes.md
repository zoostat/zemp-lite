# Zemp Lite 0.2.1 — Three panes on every place: release notes (draft)

_Draft of 22 November 2026, written at step 9. It is finished at "Release 0.2.1", after the
acceptance tests are gone through again (step 10), the reader test has been run and the owner has
tried the alpha at its address (step 11). Until then the sections marked "at the close" are open._

## What it is

The first patch of the alpha, on the same engine, the released Zeff 0.8.0 (commit
`6bfa6c55a5584e4e038fc828fef900a30f0856c3`, tag `v0.8.0`): one layout in place of two, decided by
the owner on 21 November 2026 (open decision #203). Its address is `https://lite.zeff-lang.dev`.

## What shipped

**One layout: three panes on every place.** Every place has a left pane, the place itself in the
middle and a right pane, each opened and closed by its own link in a bar above it, and by its own
×; the edge of a pane is dragged, or moved by the arrow keys; each link is also a command of ⌘K.
Each place remembers which of its panes are open, with the project. The **Margin** and **Panes**
links are gone, and with Panes its Output panel, the editor's tabs and the Objects panel.

**Script.** At the left **Project** (the project's files; a click opens a script) and **Outline**,
both closed at first; at the right **Notes**, open at first: the note beside each statement, which
says what it made. **Outline** lists the sections of the script, a section begun by a comment
`// # Title` and a subsection by `// ## Title`; a click brings a section to the top, the section
of the cursor is marked, and **Run section** (on each entry, and in ⌘K) runs a section's
statements with what they need. **Run** unfolds every output under its statement, each as its
statement ends; a run of part of the script (⌘↵, Run to here, Run from here, Run section) unfolds
what was asked for and leaves the rest as it was. The code's column takes 100 characters, the
longest line Zeff's formatter leaves, beside the notes.

**Data.** At the left **Frames of the run**; at the right **Variables**, closed until asked for or
until a variable is chosen. The pane shows the list of the frame's variables (each shown in the
grid or hidden) or, in its place, one variable: its label and type, its rows and how many have no
value with their share, its distribution, and its statistics by its type: for a number N, mean,
sd, min, the quartiles and median, max and the distinct values; for a variable of few values each
value's count and share; for a date or a time its least and greatest. Every number is a cell of
what the engine prints (`summary`, `quantile`, `tabulate`, `n_distinct`, `min`, `max`), on the
frame as the view shows it, so a condition's statistics are its own. The pane is for looking: a
variable is changed in the script.

**Plots.** At the left **Plots of the run**; at the right **The plot**: its statement, coloured as
code, its line (a link to the script), its kind and the size it is saved at.

**Help**, a place of its own, the fourth of the top bar: the engine's page in the middle, the
page's outline at the left and its related functions at the right, each with what it does. F1 on a
name, **Explain** in what a run said, and a field open a page.

**Mended.** In 0.2 the notes were drawn 300 pixels too far right, outside the page, so that a
reader saw none: they stand beside their statements. An unfolded table no longer keeps the page
from narrowing with the window. A file being saved while the project was first read could leave
the window without hover, completion, help and explained pages until it was reloaded: no longer.
Rows asked under a condition while a run starts are no longer answered with the whole frame.

## Breaking changes

For a user's project, none: the views file is read as before (now version 3; versions 1 and 2 are
read, and what they said of the layouts that are gone is passed over). A project opens in the one
layout, with Project and Outline closed and Notes open, whatever layout it was left in. What is no
longer there:

- the Panes layout, with its Output panel and the editor's tabs: every output is under its
  statement, and a second file is opened from **Project** or ⌘K;
- the Objects panel: what a statement made is in its note, and a frame is looked at in Data;
- the Help panel beside the script, and its switch that followed the cursor: Help is a place;
- in Data, **Copy as Zeff** for a change of a variable's type, contrast or labels: the statement
  is written in the script (`to`, `category`, `label`). **Copy as Zeff** for a sort or a condition
  stays.

The Scratch project's two opening comments say what Run now does; a Scratch project made before
keeps its own text.

## Reference versions

As in 0.2: every output is held to what the released Zeff 0.8.0 command line prints, writes and
says for the same script, byte for byte; no other software is a reference for Zemp. New at this
release: the 24 statements the Variables pane asks of the engine, each with what `zeff run` prints
for it (`tests/scripts/properties.json`).

## What is left out, and why

As in 0.2, with this release's own: the sections of a script are read by the interface from its
comments until the engine's language server reports them (#178, Zeff 0.9.0); a variable's
statistics are asked by running the script again with the statements after it, since the engine of
0.8.0 keeps nothing between two runs (C14); no layout for a window narrower than 1,200 pixels
(#33).

## Known issues

- The issues of 0.2 stand, but the first: the notes are where they belong.
- Visits from Safari may go uncounted: WebKit refuses the counter's report to Cloudflare's Web
  Analytics under its access checks, and the report is Cloudflare's own script, not this
  repository's to mend. Nothing else is affected, and nothing about the visitor is sent either way.
- A plot drawn to fit is at most 1,100 pixels wide: in a wide window with both panes of Plots
  closed it does not fill the place.
- A help page is as wide as the engine prints it (118 characters): with both panes open in a
  narrow window it is read by scrolling sideways, or with a pane closed.

## The design's acceptance tests (its section 21), gone through again in the one layout

Each test of 0.2's notes, with the test that holds it now. _Holds_ names the test; _not built_
says what it waits for; _by hand_ is checked on the hosted alpha at step 11.

**Principles.**

- Running inserts and moves no text: holds (`margin.spec`, "each statement's output is directly
  under it, and the script itself is not changed"; `figure.spec`, "the lines added to draw the
  figures never show").
- The opened result is what `zeff run --width n` prints, and Copy as text what `zeff run` prints:
  holds (`margin.spec`, "the engine prints at the width of the place, and again at a new width
  when it changes" and "Copy as text gives what `zeff run` prints"; the native tests at each
  width), and now for Run alone, which unfolds every output (`margin.spec`, "a script opens with
  no output; Run puts a note beside each statement and unfolds every output"; `output.spec`,
  `page.spec`, `first.spec` and `samples.spec` at 118 characters).
- No number in a note formatted by the interface: holds (`notes.node.test`). The same now of the
  Variables pane: holds (`properties.node.test`; the native test of
  `tests/scripts/properties.json`; `data.spec`, the four tests of a variable's properties).
- No control writes to the script but the console line's copy and **Format the script**: holds
  (`data.spec`, "the Variables pane is for looking: nothing in it changes a variable or offers
  the statement that would"; `console.spec`).
- View changes alter no export or result: holds for the data viewer's hiding (`data.spec`) and
  for the panes, which are view state alone (`kept.spec`, "which panes of a place are open, and
  the left pane's width, are kept with the project"); the rest by hand.

**Script.**

- A script opens with no output: holds. That Run leaves every output folded is revised by #203:
  Run unfolds every output, and a run of part what was asked for (`margin.spec`, the test above
  and "a run of part of the script unfolds the outputs of the statements asked for, and no
  others").
- One note per statement, no estimate in a note, an output holding only what the engine said and
  printed, **Unfold all**, **Fold all**, **Notes**, several open at once: hold (`margin.spec`, "a
  note unfolds its statement's output under the statement, and folds it again", "Unfold all is
  every output under its statement; Fold all folds all but an error", "Notes hides and shows the
  notes, and changes nothing else").
- A note stands beside its statement, in the page: holds now (`panes.spec`, "a note stands
  inside the page, in the 300 pixels at the right of its statement's line"); in 0.2 it did not,
  and no test asked.
- Each step of a chain its own note: not built (C5). Staleness exactly as the engine would
  reuse: not built, the safe rule (`margin.spec`, "an edit marks its statement edited and what
  stands below it stale; a comment marks nothing"). A console statement marks nothing: by hand.
- ⌘↵ on a statement whose input is stale runs that input first: holds (`margin.spec`, "⌘↵ runs
  the statement at the cursor with what it needs, and leaves the rest as it was").
- A refused script runs nothing and shows the engine's message under its line: holds
  (`check.spec`; `margin.spec`, "when the check refuses a script, the results of the run before
  stay on the page"; `output.spec`, "a refused script leaves the results of the run before, and
  the run's facts, as they were").
- Every warning in the margin and the list with the same code, sentence and line: holds
  (`margin.spec`, "what the run said lists every message with its code, sentence and line, as
  the margin has them").
- Section comments, the outline and **Run section**: built here, in the interface's reading of
  the comment (`sections.test`; `outline.spec`, its five tests). A comparison with an earlier
  run: not built.

**Finding a fault.** As in 0.2, each by the same test of `margin.spec`: an error's trail ("an
error raised inside a function has its trail, each link a way to its line"); a loop's passes ("a
loop's output carries its passes…", "a loop that fails ends its passes on the pass the engine
names"); **Run to here** over a line number and in ⌘K ("Run to here, on a statement's line
number, runs the statements before it"; `palette.spec`, "every control of the window can be
reached from the keyboard", which holds the command). 200,000 passes in under 100 ms: by hand.
The passes searched, a call opened, the console line inside a call: not built (R16 to R18, C23).

**The print at every size.** A fit's table read in full at 1,080 and 1,320 pixels, at 200% zoom,
at the largest code size, in the dark theme, with a term 40 characters long: by hand.

**Places.**

- What Data and Plots show names its line: holds (`data.spec`, "the frames of the run are listed
  by the statement that made them, in the left pane"; `viewer.spec`, "the Plots place: the plots
  of the run at its left and the plot's facts at its right, each closed by its link").
- A missing value never blank: holds (`data.spec`). A row left out naming its condition: not
  built. `report()` notes on columns that differ, and `coef_plot()`: the engine's, not in 0.8.0.
- A figure the engine's, never stretched: holds (`figure.spec`, "when its place is made narrower
  a plot is laid out afresh, never stretched, its text the same size").
- Unticking a variable, and hiding kept for each frame: hold (`data.spec`). A list that hides:
  now each pane's link and its × (`panes.spec`, `data.spec`, `viewer.spec`, `help.spec`).
- **The console line's plots listed apart from the script's: not built.** 0.2's notes said this
  held, by `viewer.spec`; going through the tests again found no test of it and nothing in the
  list of plots that knows a plot of the console line. The list holds the plots of the script's
  run. It is recorded here as not built, and 0.2's notes carry the correction.

**Paper.** Not in this alpha (#141).

**Accessibility and scope.** Text at 4.5 to 1 or more: holds for every pair of the colours
(`tokens.test`), the code shown in the Plots place standing on the window's ground, which its
colours are held to. None under 12.5 px: holds (`workspace.spec`, "app screens are set at 13.5
pixels and never below 12.5", now with both left panes open and a run's notes and outputs on the
page). Every control a stop of the Tab key with a name, the edge of a pane among them, and
every pane's link a command of ⌘K: holds (`palette.spec`; `panes.spec`, "each pane's link is a
command of ⌘K"); the keyboard alone through a whole session: by hand. A screen reader learns
each statement's state, the warnings and what is stale: by hand, with VoiceOver.

## Measures

- The engine's worker: 15.1 MB, **2.92 MB** with brotli as it travels (the limit of risk R4:
  5 MB); a cold start under Node of 59 ms (2,920.0 kB; 0.2: 2,919.9 kB and 55 ms).
- The first start at `https://lite.zeff-lang.dev` from GitHub's machines, over this release's
  deployments: 1.2 to 1.4 s in Chromium, 1.8 to 2.5 s in WebKit (0.2: 1.4 s and 2.0 s; the goal
  2 seconds).
- A plot drawn afresh: 160 to 290 ms in Chromium and 100 to 130 ms in WebKit at the address from
  GitHub's machines (0.2: 160 to 175 ms).
- ⌘↵ on the last statement of the returns to schooling: tens of milliseconds, in both engines.
  0.2's notes gave 2.2 s for Chromium and did not trust WebKit's 40 ms; the run's own facts
  settle it: the engine runs the statement with the two frames it reads in 6 ms, and the key to
  "done" is 18 ms (Chromium, at the address, from this Mac). The 2.2 s was the page drawing its
  figures after the run, which that measure took in. The check after each deployment now folds
  the outputs first, holds that the last line is the script's last, and reports both times.
