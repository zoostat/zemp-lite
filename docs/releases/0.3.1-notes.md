# Zemp Lite 0.3.1 — Zemp Lite redrawn: the revamp, cobalt, and computers only: release notes

_Released 28 November 2026, on the owner's word ("Release 0.3.1")._

## What it is

The preview redrawn, on the same engine, the released Zeff 0.9.0 (commit
`670e73f8afc6c741a1f65a652c957281531108e9`, tag `v0.9.0`). Three rulings of the owner of 27
November 2026, placed by him at this release on 28 November, are built: Zemp Lite for computers
only (open decision #243); cobalt in place of the logo's violet (#244); and the revamp as the
mockup of that day draws it (#245, with #33 amended for the side menu): the front page, the
projects screen, one slim top bar and one thin line below, a side menu of places, and each
place laid out for the most room. Nothing the engine computes has changed: every number is as
at 0.3. Its address is `https://lite.zeff-lang.dev`.

## What changed

- **Computers only** (#243). A window narrower than 1,000 pixels is shown a page that says
  Zemp Lite is made for a computer, with **Go on anyway**. The layouts for a phone are gone. A
  tablet held sideways is a small laptop, and every screen is tested at 1,024 × 768. Where the
  pointer is a finger, whatever is pressed is at least 32 pixels.
- **Cobalt** (#244). The logo, the Lite badge, the filled button, links and selections; in
  code, a function's name is teal and an operator slate grey; the greys are cooler.
- **The frame** (#245; #33 amended). One top bar of 38 pixels: **Projects ▾**, the project and
  the file with `saved`, the field of commands, the code's size, **Dark**, **Check**, **Run**.
  A side menu of five icons, Script, Data, Plots, Paper and Help, with **All projects** at its
  foot; each says its name, what it holds and its key on hover and on focus, and ⌘1 to ⌘5 open
  them. One line at the foot: the console's field, how the results stand to the script, the
  run's line, the warnings, the reminder on where the work is kept, and the engine.
- **Script.** One row: **Project**, **Outline**, **Notes**, the run's line, **Export script
  with output…**, **Fold all**, **Unfold all**. A result's head is one line. **Explain** opens
  the code's page in Help, which links back to the line that raised it. A note leads to Data
  or to Plots. Resting the pointer on a function's name, or ⇧F1, shows its card: what it does,
  its usage, and what it matches in R and Stata.
- **Data.** One row: the frames as a menu, the frame's facts, **Data** and **Variables**, the
  two fields and **Copy as Zeff**. Rows of 25 pixels. A column is selected by its head, and a
  line under the table gives its summary.
- **Plots.** The run's plots as a strip in the row; the sizes and **Pop out** beside it; the
  plot under the row; a panel at the right with its statement, the format and **Save…**.
- **Paper.** A row that says the paper and where its tables and figures come from, with **New
  paper…** and **Export PDF…** at its end; the Outline lists the tables and figures.
- **Help.** The index at the left: the search field at its top, **Start here**, **Packages**,
  **Functions** (every function by its name), **Errors and warnings**, **Using Zemp Lite**.
  **See also** beside a page. **Try in the console** under an example.
- **The projects screen.** A column of what can be done, with the note on where the work is
  kept, the room used and **Export all projects…**; the projects as a table, with how each
  last ran, **Open**, **Export…** and a menu on each row; a field that finds a project; three
  cards.
- **The front page.** "Reproducible empirical research, _in your browser._", **Start**, three
  facts, and the engine's card with three lines of script and what the engine prints for them.
- **Using Zemp Lite.** Its fourteen pages say the new screens, and their fourteen pictures are
  made again.

## The rulings

#243, #244 and #245 of the open-decisions register, decided on 27 November 2026 and placed at
this release on 28 November, with the owner's answer to the plan's questions ("Yes, all
three"): Plots keeps every way of laying out it had; the watermark stays, faint, behind the
front page and the projects screen; **Try in the console** joins **Run** and **Copy to
script**. #33 is amended for the side menu alone: its entries are icons, named on hover and on
focus. Every engineering choice made on the way is in `docs/decisions.md`, a section for each
step.

Where the app says other words than the mockup, because the mockup said more than holds:

- The front page's first fact does not say "works offline once loaded": the app keeps the
  engine but not its own page for a visit with no network.
- The front page's example is three lines whose whole print is one number; the mockup drew a
  regression's table cut to two lines and five columns, which would not be the engine's print.
- On the projects screen, the card on R and Stata says "most pages say what they match":
  64 of the engine's 266 pages have no such section. The card on `reg` leaves out "checked
  against R".
- Help's **Start here** has **Finding help** where the mockup has a page "Coming from R or
  Stata", which does not exist.

## Breaking changes

None for a project's files, and none from the engine, which is the same. On screen:

- A window narrower than 1,000 pixels no longer has a layout of its own: it is shown the page
  that says so, and may go on.
- The places are icons at the left, not words in the top bar; the search of Help is at the top
  of its index, not in its bar; the plots of a run are a strip across the top, not a pane at
  the left; the list of frames is a menu in Data's row, not a pane.
- The view kept with a project (`.zemp/views.json`) is at version 6. A view of an earlier
  version is read, and the panes that no longer exist are passed over.
- The browser now remembers, beside when each project was opened and exported, how its last
  run went. It is this browser's memory: a project opened from a zip says "not run".

## The tests replaced, and why

- The tests of a phone's and an upright tablet's layout (in twelve files) are replaced by
  `computer.spec.ts`: the page a narrow window is shown, **Go on anyway**, and the size of what
  a finger presses. #243 withdrew those layouts.
- The whole-screen suite is run at 1,024 × 768 in both themes (`whole-1024.spec.ts`), where the
  plan's draft asked for every test twice: every place, in each of its states, is held to the
  window, to 12.5 pixels and to 4.5 to 1.
- The tests of the places in the top bar, of the pane of plots, of the pane of frames, of the
  search in Help's bar, of the four cards and the Recent list, and of the loading screen's name
  and band are rewritten for what stands in their place (`frame`, `viewer`, `data`,
  `help-search`, `welcome`, `start`).
- No test was weakened to pass. Three of mine were wrong and were corrected when GitHub's
  slower machines showed it: a script read from a window too short to draw it (step 3, seen at
  step 4); a count of examples run again after a page was narrowed in two moves (step 3, seen
  at step 8b); four pages of Help asked for with no wait between them (step 11b). And one check
  made only against the published address was not updated at step 10, which stopped that
  step's deployment before the public address, as the check is meant to; from step 12b those
  checks are run on the owner's Mac against the built app before each commit.

## Reference versions

As at 0.3: every output is held to what the released Zeff 0.9.0 command line prints, writes and
says for the same script, byte for byte: the ten sample projects, the Scratch project's four
scripts, the code of the pages of **Using Zemp Lite**, every example of Help, and now the three
lines of the front page. A paper and a script's PDF are set by Typst 0.15.1.

## Known issues

- **Export all projects…** saves one zip for each project, one after another; a browser may
  ask, once, whether the site may save several files.
- The app does not work with no network: the browser keeps the engine, but the page itself is
  fetched at each visit.
- How a project's last run went is remembered by the browser in which it ran; a project opened
  from a zip, or in another browser, says "not run" until it is run there.
- The card of a function says what it matches in R and Stata from the engine's catalogue of
  names where the function's page has no such section (64 of 266 pages, `reg`'s among them).
- A figure's number in a paper's Outline is counted in the order of the paper; a paper that
  sets a figure's counter by hand would be numbered otherwise on its page.
- ⌘1 to ⌘5 are also a browser's keys for its own tabs, and a browser may keep them; each place
  is in the list of commands too.
- Carried from 0.3: which files a script would write is read from its text; the examples of
  the explained codes are illustrations and are not run as their page opens; hyphenation in
  four languages may differ from the desktop Typst's; the page of a paper is a picture.
- Drawn in the mockup and not built, each for a ruling of its own: a favicon; ⇧⌘E; the
  coloured dots of a note's kind; "Past runs" in the Project panel.
- Not run or not checked at this release: the reader test (moved by the owner to Zeff 0.11.0,
  #230) and the checks by hand (the keyboard alone on a real keyboard, VoiceOver, Safari
  itself, and ⌘1 to ⌘5 in the owner's own browser).

## To try it

Open `https://lite.zeff-lang.dev` in Chrome, Edge or Safari on a computer. The front page
starts the engine; press **Start**, or Return. On the projects screen, press **Scratch
project** and then **Run**. Point at the icons down the left, or press ⌘1 to ⌘5. In **Data**,
press a column's head. In **Plots**, tick **Compare** on both plots. In **Help**, type `lm` in
the field at the top of the index, open `reg`, and press **Try in the console** under an
example. Back on the projects screen (**All projects**), the table says how the run went. Make
the window narrower than 1,000 pixels to see the page it is then shown. The address is not
announced until the trademarks are filed (#37).
